"Every AI governance tool gives you a dashboard. None give you proof. The ADR is the missing evidentiary layer."

The Agent Decision Record is an open specification for creating, storing, and verifying tamper-evident records of AI system decisions. It defines the minimum required fields, cryptographic integrity requirements, chain of custody standards, and evidentiary metadata needed for an AI decision record to function as audit-ready evidence before regulators, courts, and coalition compliance bodies.

This specification forms the foundation of the accountability.ai measurement infrastructure. It is published as an open standard that any organization may implement.

01 Executive Summary

1.1 The ADR in Plain Language

The Agent Decision Record (ADR) is a structured and cryptographically verifiable record generated at the moment an AI system produces a consequential output. It captures what the system decided, who authorized the decision, under what policy, on what evidence, and with what reasoning. All elements are bound into a tamper-evident chain that any party can independently verify.

The ADR addresses a gap that existing AI governance tools have not closed. There is a difference between a system that appears accountable and one that can prove accountability to a regulator, a court, or a coalition partner. Appearances are not evidence. The ADR is.

This specification defines the minimum fields, cryptographic requirements, and evidentiary standards that an ADR implementation must satisfy. It is intentionally scoped to what is necessary, not what is convenient. The purpose is to produce records that withstand adversarial scrutiny, not records that are merely easy to generate.

1.2 Core Principle

📌 Core Principle The ADR is not a logging format. It is an evidentiary artifact.

The distinction matters. Logs capture what happened for operational purposes. An evidentiary artifact is constructed to survive challenge. Every design decision in this specification reflects that requirement.

02 Background and Motivation

2.1 The Evidentiary Gap

AI systems now make consequential decisions at scale: credit approvals, medical triage, export control screening, hiring shortlists, bail recommendations, and content moderation.

In each domain, decisions may be challenged. Challenges come from regulators investigating disparate impact, from plaintiffs alleging discrimination, from coalition partners requiring compliance verification, and from boards demanding accountability after adverse outcomes.

When a challenge arrives, the question is consistent: Can you prove what your system decided, why it decided it, and that the record has not been altered since? At most organizations today, the answer is no.

This is not necessarily due to wrongdoing. It is because the infrastructure required to produce that proof does not exist. Logs exist. Dashboards exist. Audit reports exist. None of these constitute evidentiary proof.

03 Specification Scope

This specification defines the minimum required structure for a compliant ADR. It does not prescribe implementation technology, storage architecture, or programming language. Any system that produces records conforming to the field definitions, cryptographic requirements, and evidentiary standards defined herein is a conformant ADR implementation.

  • What fields an ADR must contain (Core Field Definitions, Section 04)
  • How cryptographic integrity must be established and verified (Section 05)
  • How chain of custody must be maintained across sequential decisions (Section 06)
  • What constitutes compliant reasoning capture (Section 07)
  • How human oversight must be recorded when it occurs (Section 08)
  • How the specification maps to specific regulatory obligations (Section 09)

This specification intentionally does not define how AI systems make decisions, what decisions are permissible, or what outcomes are acceptable. Those questions are for regulators, ethics bodies, and the organizations deploying AI systems. The ADR records what happened. It does not judge it.

04 Core Field Definitions

The following fields are required in every conformant ADR. Fields marked REQUIRED must be present and non-null. Fields marked CONDITIONAL are required when the specified condition is met.

4.1 Identity Fields

FieldTypeReq.Description
adr_idstringREQUIREDGlobally unique identifier. Time-ordered (ULID recommended). Format: adr_{26-char-ulid}. Immutable after generation.
timestampISO 8601REQUIREDUTC timestamp of decision generation at inference time. Must reflect when the decision was produced, not when the record was written.
agent_idstringREQUIREDStable identifier of the AI system or model instance. Must correspond to a registered system in the agent registry.
agent_versionstringREQUIREDExact version string of the deployed model. Must be sufficient to reproduce the model state at decision time.

4.2 Decision Fields

FieldTypeReq.Description
decision_typestringREQUIREDClassification of the decision domain. Examples: credit_approval, clinical_triage, export_screen, hiring_screen.
input_summaryobjectREQUIREDPseudonymized summary of inputs. Must not contain raw PII. Must contain sufficient context to reconstruct the decision.
outputobjectREQUIREDExact output produced by the AI system, verbatim. No post-processing or summarization. Includes decision, confidence, and structured metadata.
confidencenumberREQUIREDConfidence score as a percentage (0–100). Must reflect the system's internal certainty metric, not a post-hoc assignment.

4.3 Reasoning Fields

The reasoning field is the most legally significant field in the ADR. It must be captured at inference time. Post-hoc reconstruction of reasoning is not compliant with this specification and is insufficient as evidentiary documentation.

FieldTypeReq.Description
reasoningstringREQUIREDHuman-readable explanation of why the AI system produced this output. Minimum 20 characters. Must be captured at inference, not reconstructed after the fact.
reasoning_methodstringCONDITIONALMethod used to generate the reasoning explanation. Values: chain_of_thought, shap, lime, rule_trace, attention. Required when confidence < 80%.
feature_attributionobjectCONDITIONALFeature-level weights contributing to the decision. Required for high-risk AI systems. Format: {feature_name: weight}.

4.4 Policy and Authority Fields

FieldTypeReq.Description
policy_versionstringREQUIREDVersion identifier of the governing policy at inference time. Immutable. Policy changes require a new version string.
jurisdictionstring[]REQUIREDArray of jurisdiction identifiers. Examples: US-ECOA, EU-AIAct-HighRisk, Pax-Silica-v1.
human_review_requiredbooleanREQUIREDWhether this decision requires human review before action. Determined by policy at inference time, not assigned after the decision.
risk_classificationstringREQUIREDRisk tier of this decision. Values: unacceptable, high, limited, minimal.
affected_party_idstringCONDITIONALPseudonymized identifier of the affected party. Required for decisions affecting identifiable individuals. Never store raw PII.

4.5 Extension Fields

Optional but strongly recommended for high-risk AI systems.

FieldTypeReq.Description
data_quality_flagsobjectOPTIONALFlags indicating data quality issues at decision time. Example: {source_stale: true, freshness_hours: 31, threshold_hours: 24}.
agent_chainstring[]OPTIONALArray of adr_ids from upstream AI agents. Required for multi-agent pipeline traceability.
evidence_basisobjectOPTIONALDocumentation of evidence or knowledge sources used. For RAG systems: document IDs, retrieval timestamps, relevance scores.
coalition_complianceobjectOPTIONALPax Silica coalition compliance metadata. Enables cross-border verification without sharing underlying decision data.
remediation_flagbooleanOPTIONALIndicates this decision has been flagged for remediation review. Once set to true, immutable.

05 Cryptographic Integrity Requirements

Every ADR must include three cryptographic elements: a record hash, a previous hash linking it to the prior record in the chain, and a signature binding it to the generating infrastructure. Together these make the record tamper-evident and independently verifiable.

FieldTypeReq.Description
record_hashstringREQUIREDSHA-256 hash of the canonical serialization of this ADR. Exact field ordering specified to ensure deterministic reproduction.
previous_hashstringREQUIREDThe record_hash of the preceding ADR in this agent_id chain. Genesis record uses SHA256("ADR-GENESIS-{agent_id}-{date}").
signaturestringREQUIREDEd25519 digital signature of the record_hash, produced by the infrastructure key registered for this agent. Enables third-party verification.
chain_sequenceintegerREQUIREDMonotonically increasing integer per agent_id. Gaps in sequence indicate deleted records. Combined with previous_hash for dual tamper evidence.

5.1 Hash Chain Integrity

H(n) = SHA256( Payload(n) ‖ H(n − 1) )

Where H(n) is the record hash, Payload(n) is the canonical serialization, and ‖ denotes concatenation.

5.2 Canonical Serialization

The canonical form serializes fields in the order defined in Section 04, with no whitespace, using UTF-8 encoding. Fields included in the canonical hash are: adr_id, timestamp, agent_id, agent_version, decision_type, output, confidence, reasoning, policy_version, jurisdiction, previous_hash, chain_sequence. Extension fields are excluded to preserve forward compatibility.

5.3 Key Management

Production implementations must use Ed25519 key pairs managed by a hardware security module (HSM) or equivalent. The public key must be published at a well-known endpoint to enable third-party verification. Key rotation must produce a new genesis record in the chain.

06 Chain of Custody

The ADR chain of custody is established through the combination of sequential chain_sequence integers and cryptographic hash linkage. Every record references the hash of its predecessor, making any alteration or deletion detectable.

6.1 Chain Structure

ADR(n−1) → ADR(n) → ADR(n+1)
seq: n−1    seq: n    seq: n+1
prev_hash → prev_hash → …

6.2 Tamper Detection

  • Deletion of a record: creates a gap in chain_sequence. Detectable by any verifier.
  • Alteration of a record: changes record_hash, breaking the hash link to the successor. Detectable by any verifier.
  • Insertion of a fabricated record: requires re-signing with the infrastructure key. Detectable if the public key is registered.
  • Alteration of reasoning after the fact: changes record_hash. This is the primary protection against post-hoc rationalization.

6.3 Immutability Requirements

Conformant ADR storage must enforce append-only behavior. No UPDATE or DELETE operations are permitted on ledger records after insertion. This must be enforced at the storage layer, not the application layer. Row-level security policies, WORM storage, or equivalent controls satisfy this requirement.

The immutability requirement is not a technical preference. It is the foundation of the evidentiary standard. A record that can be altered is not evidence. The absence of update and delete permissions must be verifiable by auditors.

07 Reasoning Capture Standards

The reasoning field is the most frequently challenged field in an adversarial examination. The following standards govern what constitutes compliant reasoning capture.

7.1 Contemporaneous Requirement

Reasoning must be captured at the moment of inference, as part of the same transaction that produces the decision output. Reasoning generated after the fact—even if accurate—does not satisfy this requirement. The timestamp of the reasoning must match the timestamp of the decision.

7.2 Minimum Content Standard

A compliant reasoning field must:

  • State what the AI system decided and at what confidence level.
  • Identify the primary factors that drove the decision, with direction (supporting or opposing).
  • Flag any data quality issues present at decision time.
  • Identify whether a near-miss condition existed (decision within threshold of an alternative outcome).
  • State the policy basis for the decision and any human review requirement.

A reasoning field that states only "approved" or "denied" is not compliant. A reasoning field that reconstructs a plausible explanation after the fact is not compliant and constitutes a falsification of the evidentiary record.

08 Human Oversight Record

When a human reviewer intervenes in an AI decision, that intervention must be recorded as a separate artifact linked to the originating ADR. The human oversight record preserves the original AI output even when the human modifies or overrides it.

FieldTypeReq.Description
ledger_event_iduuidREQUIREDReference to the ADR that triggered this human review.
reviewer_idstringREQUIREDPseudonymized identifier of the human reviewer. Must be traceable to a named individual in a separate, access-controlled registry.
outcomestringREQUIREDResult of human review. Values: approved, denied, modified, escalated.
original_outputobjectREQUIREDThe AI system's original output before human modification. Must be preserved verbatim even when overridden.
modified_outputobjectCONDITIONALThe human-modified output. Required when outcome is modified. Records the human's intervention verbatim.
human_override_deltaobjectCONDITIONALStructured diff between original_output and modified_output. Captures direction and magnitude of human correction. Required when outcome is modified.
reviewer_notesstringOPTIONALFree-text notes documenting the basis for the reviewer's decision. Strongly recommended for modified and escalated outcomes.
reviewed_atISO 8601REQUIREDUTC timestamp of human review completion. This is the legal timestamp of the human intervention.
review_duration_secsintegerOPTIONALTime elapsed between assignment and completion of review. Used to detect rubber-stamping patterns in compliance audits.

10 Versioning and Governance

The ADR specification uses semantic versioning.

  • Major version increments indicate breaking changes to field structure or cryptographic requirements.
  • Minor version increments add new fields or clarify existing ones in a backward-compatible manner.
  • Patch version increments are editorial corrections.

This document is version 0.1, a founding draft released for public comment. Transition to version 1.0 will occur only after:

  • A public comment period of no less than 90 days.
  • Review by at least three independent regulatory experts.
  • Pilot implementation by at least three organizations across different sectors.

10.1 Governance Structure

Governance of the specification will transition to a multi-stakeholder body prior to version 1.0. The structure will ensure that no single organization, including accountability.ai, controls the standard. The standard must be owned by the ecosystem it serves.

10.2 How to Engage

This specification is published for public comment. Input is sought from:

  • Regulatory practitioners and former examiners who can stress-test the evidentiary standards.
  • Technical implementers who can identify gaps in the cryptographic and systems design.
  • Organizations planning to implement the ADR who can identify practical barriers.
  • Legal practitioners who can evaluate the admissibility framework.
